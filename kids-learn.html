🌍 The Water Cycle

Water never stops moving. Tap each step to learn more about where water goes on its endless journey.

1☀️

Evaporation

The sun heats oceans and lakes until water rises as invisible vapor.

2☁️

Condensation

High in the sky the vapor cools and turns into droplets that form clouds.

3🌧️

Precipitation

Clouds get heavy and water falls back down as rain, snow, sleet, or hail.

4🏔️

Collection

Water gathers in oceans, rivers, and lakes — or soaks down into the ground.

5🌱

Transpiration

Plants drink water through roots and breathe vapor out through their leaves.

🦕 Mind-Blowing Thought

Because water keeps cycling forever, the water in your glass right now has been around for billions of years. Some of it fell as rain on dinosaurs. Some of it was once in a cloud over the ocean. There is no such thing as brand-new water!

⭐ Amazing Water Facts

Tap a card to flip it over and reveal the fact hiding underneath!

🧊

Ice Is a Weirdo

Almost every substance gets denser when it freezes — but water gets LESS dense. That's why ice floats, and why fish can survive under a frozen pond.

🫧

Water Sticks to Itself

Water molecules grip each other so tightly it creates "surface tension" — strong enough for some insects to walk right across a pond.

🌡️

Water Holds Heat

Water warms up and cools down slowly. That's why oceans keep coastal towns milder, and why a pool stays cold long after a hot day starts.

🚀

Water Is in Space

Scientists have found frozen water on Mars, on moons like Europa and Enceladus, and even inside comets zooming through space.

🧪 Try It Yourself: Real Experiments

Grab a grown-up and some kitchen supplies. These are real science experiments you can do today.

🛍️

Water Cycle in a Bag

You need: a zip-top bag, water, blue food coloring, tape, a sunny window.

  1. Pour about a quarter cup of water into the bag and add a drop of food coloring.
  2. Seal it tightly and draw a sun, clouds, and waves on the bag with a marker.
  3. Tape it to a sunny window and wait a few hours.
  4. Watch droplets form at the top and "rain" back down — that's the whole water cycle!
🧫

Build a Water Filter

You need: an empty bottle, coffee filter, sand, small gravel, cotton, muddy water.

  1. Have an adult cut the bottom off the bottle and flip it upside down.
  2. Layer inside: coffee filter, then cotton, then sand, then gravel on top.
  3. Slowly pour muddy water through and catch it in a cup below.
  4. Compare before and after! Do not drink it — filtering removes dirt, not germs.
🌸

Color-Changing Flowers

You need: white carnations or celery, cups, water, food coloring.

  1. Fill cups with water and add lots of food coloring to each.
  2. Put a white flower or celery stalk in each cup.
  3. Check back every few hours for two days.
  4. The petals change color — you're watching plants drink water and transpire!
🪙

How Many Drops on a Penny?

You need: a penny, an eyedropper or straw, water, dish soap.

  1. Guess how many water drops will fit on a penny. Write your guess down.
  2. Add drops one at a time and count until the water spills.
  3. Most people are shocked — it's usually 20 or more!
  4. Now try again with soapy water. Fewer drops fit, because soap breaks surface tension.
☀️

Sun vs. Shade Race

You need: two identical cups, water, a marker.

  1. Fill both cups with the same amount of water and mark the level.
  2. Put one in bright sun and one in the shade.
  3. Check the levels after a full day.
  4. The sunny cup lost more water — that's evaporation powered by the sun!
🕵️

Home Water Detective

You need: paper, pencil, and sharp eyes.

  1. Walk through your home and list every place water is used.
  2. Check each faucet and toilet for drips or running water.
  3. Time how long your shower takes for one week.
  4. Pick ONE change to try — shorter showers, or turning off the tap — and track how it goes.

⚠️ Safety First, Scientist!

Always have an adult help with cutting, hot water, or anything involving glass. Never drink water used in an experiment — even if it looks clean, filtering does not remove germs.

📖 Words About Water

Water shows up all through the Bible — as a gift, a promise, and a picture of life itself.

🌊

"Come, everyone who thirsts, come to the waters..."

Isaiah 55:1 (ESV)

Just like a cold drink on a hot day, God invites everyone to come and be filled up.

💧

"...whoever drinks of the water that I will give him will never be thirsty again."

John 4:14 (ESV)

Jesus talked about a special kind of "water" that fills your heart forever — not just your cup!

🐑

"He makes me lie down in green pastures. He leads me beside still waters."

Psalm 23:2 (ESV)

A calm, quiet river or pond can remind us that we're cared for and safe.

🌍

"And God said, 'Let the waters under the heavens be gathered together into one place, and let the dry land appear.'"

Genesis 1:9 (ESV)

On Day 3 of creation, God made oceans, lakes, and rivers — water has been part of the plan from the very beginning!

✨

"Then the angel showed me the river of the water of life, bright as crystal, flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb."

Revelation 22:1 (ESV)

The Bible ends with a picture of the cleanest, brightest river ever — sparkling like crystal!

⛰️

"...and water shall come out of it, and the people will drink."

Exodus 17:6 (ESV)

When people in the desert were thirsty, God provided water right out of a rock — a good reminder that water is a gift, not something to waste.

🌱

"He is like a tree planted by streams of water that yields its fruit in its season, and its leaf does not wither."

Psalm 1:3 (ESV)

Plants near water grow strong and healthy — a picture of what happens when we stay close to good things.

🕊️

"...the waters subsided from the earth continually..."

Genesis 8:3 (ESV)

After the flood, the waters went back down and life started fresh — water can reshape the whole world.
