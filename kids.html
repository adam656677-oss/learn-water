📖 Words About Water

Water shows up all through the Bible — as a gift, a promise, and a picture of life itself. Here are a few favorite verses to read together.

🌊

"Come, everyone who thirsts, come to the waters..."

Isaiah 55:1 (ESV)

Just like a cold drink on a hot day, God invites everyone to come and be filled up.

💧

"...whoever drinks of the water that I will give him will never be thirsty again. The water that I will give him will become in him a spring of water welling up to eternal life."

John 4:14 (ESV)

Jesus talked about a special kind of "water" that fills your heart forever — not just your cup!

🐑

"He makes me lie down in green pastures. He leads me beside still waters."

Psalm 23:2 (ESV)

A calm, quiet river or pond can remind us that we're cared for and safe.

🌍

"And God said, 'Let the waters under the heavens be gathered together into one place, and let the dry land appear.' ... God called the dry land Earth, and the waters that were gathered together he called Seas."

Genesis 1:9–10 (ESV)

On Day 3 of creation, God made oceans, lakes, and rivers — water has been part of the plan since the very beginning!

✨

"Then the angel showed me the river of the water of life, bright as crystal, flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb."

Revelation 22:1 (ESV)

The Bible ends with a picture of the cleanest, brightest river ever — sparkling like crystal!

⛰️

"...and water shall come out of it, and the people will drink."

Exodus 17:6 (ESV)

When the people in the desert were thirsty, God provided water right out of a rock — a good reminder that water is a gift, not something to waste.

🔬 Try It at Home: Water Activities

Hands-on activities help kids see water in action. Always do these with an adult nearby.

☀️

Evaporation in the Sun

Place two small cups of water in different spots — one in the sun and one in the shade. Check which one loses more water over a few hours.

Talk about how the sun's energy powers evaporation.
🛁

Mini Water Cycle in a Bag

Pour a little water into a clear zip-top bag, seal it, and tape it to a sunny window. Watch “rain” form inside as water evaporates and condenses.

Connect what you see to the water cycle steps.
⚖️

Sink or Float? Water Science

Collect small objects (like a leaf, a rock, a plastic toy, and a coin). Predict whether each will sink or float, then test them in a bowl of water.

Talk about density and why some things float (like ice) while others sink.
📋

Home Water Use Checklist

Walk around your home with a checklist and count water-using activities: brushing teeth, washing dishes, laundry, showers, and watering plants.

Mark where water could be saved and turn the ideas into a family water-saving plan.

⚠️ Safety Reminder

Always have an adult help set up and clean up experiments. Never drink water that has been used in experiments or touched by anything that might make you sick.

🌍 The Water Cycle

Water is always on the move! It travels around Earth in a never-ending cycle. Here's how it works:

1 ☀️

Evaporation

The sun heats water in oceans, lakes, and rivers. The water turns into invisible water vapor and rises into the sky.

2 ☁️

Condensation

As water vapor rises high up, it gets cooler. It turns back into tiny water droplets that form clouds!

3 🌧️

Precipitation

When clouds get heavy with water droplets, the water falls back to Earth as rain, snow, sleet, or hail.

4 🏔️

Collection

Water collects in oceans, rivers, and lakes — or soaks into the ground to become groundwater. Then the cycle starts again!

5 🌱

Transpiration

Plants drink water through their roots and release water vapor through their leaves. Plants help power the water cycle too!

📝 Try Drawing the Water Cycle

Use these steps to draw your own water cycle picture or poster:

  1. Draw the sun and label it "Evaporation" near an ocean or lake.
  2. Draw clouds above and label them "Condensation".
  3. Add rain or snow falling from clouds and label it "Precipitation".
  4. Draw rivers, lakes, and oceans and label them "Collection".
  5. Add plants and label their leaves "Transpiration".

When you're done, color your picture and explain the cycle to a friend or family member.

🤔 Did You Know?

The same water that dinosaurs drank millions of years ago is still cycling around Earth today! You might be drinking ancient dinosaur water right now! 🦕

🌟 Amazing Water Facts!

Water is one of the most incredible things on Earth — here are some fast facts to share.

🌊

Most of Earth is Water

About 71% of Earth's surface is covered by water — but only about 3% is fresh water, and most of that is frozen.

🧬

You Are Mostly Water!

Your body is about 60% water. Your brain, blood, and cells need water to work properly.

🐟

Oceans Are Full of Life

The ocean covers 70% of Earth and holds most of our water. It is home to thousands of known species — and probably millions more.

🧊

Ice Floats!

Water gets less dense when it freezes, so ice floats on liquid water. This helps fish survive under frozen lakes.
